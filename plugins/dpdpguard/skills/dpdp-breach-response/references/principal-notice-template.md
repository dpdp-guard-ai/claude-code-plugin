# Affected Data Principal Notice — Draft Template

Notification to individuals under DPDP Act §8(6) and GDPR Art.34.

Send **without delay**. Do not batch this behind the regulator filing — the two
obligations run in parallel, and the individual's ability to protect themselves
degrades by the hour.

## Email template

```
Subject: Important: a security breach affected your <Company> account

Hello <first name>,

On <date>, we discovered that <specific, plain description of what happened>.

WHAT WAS AFFECTED
Your <itemise exactly what was exposed for THIS person>.

Your <list what was NOT affected — passwords, payment details> was not
involved. <Only include this if verified. Omit the sentence entirely rather
than soften it.>

WHAT WE HAVE DONE
- <containment action, with date>
- <containment action, with date>
- We notified the Data Protection Board of India on <date>.

WHAT YOU SHOULD DO
1. <Most urgent action first — e.g. change your password at <direct link>>
2. <Next action — e.g. enable two-factor authentication>
3. <Vigilance guidance specific to the data exposed>

<If identity documents or financial data were exposed, state the concrete
protective step available to them and any support being provided.>

QUESTIONS
<Named contact>, Data Protection Officer
<monitored email> · <phone>

You may also complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

<Signature — a named human, with designation. Not "The Team".>
```

## Writing rules

**Name it a breach.** Not "an incident", not "unauthorised activity", not "a
matter affecting a limited subset of records". Individuals must understand the
message on one read.

**Be specific per recipient.** "Some user data may have been accessed" does not
let anyone act. If different cohorts were exposed differently, segment the send
— one accurate variant per cohort beats one vague message to everyone.

**Order actions by urgency, not by convenience.** The first item should be the
thing that most reduces their exposure.

**No marketing.** No promotional content, no upsell, no "meanwhile, check out".
No unsubscribe-gated information.

**No liability language.** "We take your privacy seriously" and "out of an
abundance of caution" communicate nothing and read as deflection. Cut them.

**Do not over-reassure.** Never write "there is no evidence of misuse" unless
you have actually looked for evidence of misuse and can say how. Absence of
investigation is not absence of harm.

**Language.** Send in the principal's registered communication language where
known. Under §5(3), Eighth Schedule languages must be available on request.

## Channels

| Channel | Use when |
|---|---|
| Email | Primary — permanent, detailed, forwardable |
| SMS | Sev-1, or where email is unreliable. Short + link to full notice. |
| In-app | Reinforcement on next login. Never the only channel. |
| Public notice | Where individual contact is not possible for some cohort |

Send by every channel you hold for the person. If contact details for a cohort
are missing or stale, publish a public notice and document why individual
contact failed.

## SMS variant

Keep under 320 characters and never include personal data in the body:

```
<Company>: We detected a data breach affecting your account on <date>.
Please change your password now: <short link>
Details: <notice link> · Questions: <email>
```

## Pre-send checks

- [ ] Every factual claim is verified — especially "was not affected" statements
- [ ] Cohorts segmented so each recipient reads what applies to them
- [ ] Action links are correct and live
- [ ] Contact channel is staffed and briefed before send
- [ ] No tracking pixels in the breach notice
- [ ] No PII in the SMS body
- [ ] Counsel reviewed
- [ ] A named human approved the send
- [ ] Send list matches the confirmed affected set, and the query is recorded
